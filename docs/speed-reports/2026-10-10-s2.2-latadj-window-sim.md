# s2.2 latadj pass-window in the sim (fixed handler, real-silicon FLAG model)
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Goal: "Record the passing latadj window per rate."
- Changed: none. Command: `rxsim.py --src ml/mlfull.py --baud {600,2400} --runs 12 --flag edge --latadj {0,40,...,400} --seed 3` -> logs/rxsim-latadj-sweep.log
- Result: PASS (sim only), NTSC, badlines on, +-1% sender error, 12 runs x 90 bytes per point.
  | latadj | 0 | 40 | 80 | 120 | 160 | 200 | 240 | 280 | 320 | 360 | 400 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 600 baud, runs exact /12 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 |
  | 2400 baud, runs exact /12 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 3 | 6 | **12** | **12** | **12** | 0 | 0 |
  600 baud tolerates anything (a bit is 1705 cycles). At 2400 the full-pass window is ~240-320, centre ~280, i.e. +-40 cycles (+-9% of a bit). The shipped default 275 sits in the middle.
  1200 baud was 50/50 at 275 (s2.1); window not swept.
- Surprises / PLAN CORRECTION: the plan (section 1, last paragraph, and Stage 3.2) says 275 is a VICE artifact and real NMI latency is 30-80 cycles, to expect a window around 40-100. The sim says otherwise: `latadj` has to cover TWO handler paths -- start-bit handler to the timer load, and the first-sample handler to the continuous-timer start (all later samples inherit that second latency) -- so ~270 total is expected, not ~60. The UP9600 "30 cycles" figure is for a much leaner handler. So: do NOT expect hardware to want 40-100. Still sweep on hardware (real NMI entry may differ by tens of cycles), but centre the sweep on ~280 (range 100-460), not 0-400 centred low.
